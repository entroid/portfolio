## Approach

We ran extensive research with stakeholders and users across different teams to map and understand the current process's bottlenecks. The core findings: lack of centralized visibility, unclear communication channels, and manual workflows prone to error. The results: licenses expiring without clear warning, hard to locate documentation during an inspection (specially painful for field operations), complex and extremely manual cycle management. We benchmarked the market (Fizzera, Snow License Manager, ServiceNow SAM) to identify functionality gaps before scoping the MVP.

## Key Product Decisions

**• Prioritized real-time visibility over exhaustive functionality:** the MVP was scoped to advanced license search, renewal/status alerts, document access and reports generation workflows — what research showed as the most urgent pain point, not everything the legacy system attempted to cover.

**• Designed for two personas/profiles with different needs:** Marcus (field operations, needs speed and mobile access) and Sofia (administrative, needs centralized organization and traceability) — the system had to serve both without friction for either.

**• Structured the information before designing screens:** mapped information architecture and flows in Mural from research findings, validating system logic with stakeholders before investing in wireframes or prototypes.

## Notes

- **• Layered, structured research:** stakeholder interviews → empathy map → user journey map → synthesis into core pain points (Operational Delay, Error Risk, Oversight Gap, Budget Risk) — each layer feeding the next, not standalone research.

- **• Information architecture in Mural:** flows and information hierarchy were mapped collaboratively before touching Figma, giving the team (and later, engineering) a shared model of the system.

- **• Lo-fi to hi-fi in Figma with mid-point validation:** low-fidelity sketches and wireframes to validate use cases quickly, only then iterating to high-fidelity designs — reducing costly late-stage revisions.

- **• UI designed to alleviate user pain points:** the interface was crafted to reduce friction identified in the journey map, prioritizing clarity and speed of action.

## Results

Early feedback on selected users over the MVP showed both field and administrative teams replacing manual processes and uncertainties with the new system fast and with few errors. More iterations to come would improve the product and add more features. At this point, the main lesson didn't come from adding features, but from structuring existing complexity into a flow that was clear and accessible for both user profiles.

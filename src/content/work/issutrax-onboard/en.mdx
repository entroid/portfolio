## Approach

I joined the project when design artifacts were already growing in complexity, threatening consistency as the MVP launch approached. My first step was to stabilize what existed: I audited the current mockups and built a foundational style guide and component library in Adobe XD, which directly supported the dev team during the critical weeks before release, reducing handoff ambiguity. After launch, I established a structured feedback process with the crew (regular meetings) and prioritized design changes by actual impact — tickets with the highest reported friction or critical for compliance — rather than by who asked loudest.

## Key Product Decisions

**• Prioritized stabilizing and systematizing before adding new functionality:** instead of redesigning from scratch, I built a style guide and component library from what already existed, given how close the MVP launch was.

**• Turned feedback into a repeatable process, not one-off requests:** regular crew meetings enabled prioritization by real impact (reported friction, compliance criticality) instead of reacting ticket by ticket.

**• Focused iteration on the two highest-pressure flows for the crew** (ticket creation/updating and the real-time status dashboard) instead of spreading effort evenly across the whole product.

## Notes

- **• Audit and design system in Adobe XD:** colors, typography (Roboto), buttons, grid/table components, notifications, modals, and nav menu variants — establishing a single source of truth for the design-dev handoff.

- **• Embedded in Scrum:** active participation in team ceremonies, translating technical constraints into feasible UX solutions in real time, rather than working in isolation and delivering after the fact.

- **• Post-launch iteration driven by usage data and real qualitative crew feedback**, not assumptions.

## Results

The design system reduced friction and back-and-forth questions between design and engineering. Iterations based on crew feedback led to better adoption and satisfaction among users. The systematized UI left a solid foundation for adding new functionality without breaking the application's overall consistency.

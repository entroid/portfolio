## Process

The starting point was a week of shadowing on-call engineers during actual
incidents — watching where they clicked, what they scrolled past, and which
tab they always had open in a second window because the primary dashboard
couldn't answer their first question fast enough.

/>

That research collapsed into three jobs the dashboard had to do well:
surface the right signal first, let an operator correlate events across
services without leaving the page, and make handoff between shifts a
non-event instead of a re-briefing.

## Decisions

Severity became a first-class visual dimension instead of a column value —
color and position on the timeline do the work that used to require reading
a text field. Correlation moved from "open five tabs" to an inline expandable
row, so an operator can trace a cascading failure without losing their place
in the main feed.

<CaseStudyImage
  src="/images/work/signal-desk/timeline-detail.jpg"
  alt="Close-up of the incident timeline showing a severity-coded event cluster expanded to reveal three correlated downstream service failures"
/>

The hardest call was what to leave out: an earlier draft surfaced every
available metric per event, and testing showed it made triage slower, not
faster. The shipped version defaults to the minimum signal needed to decide
"is this mine," with detail one click away rather than always-on.

## Technical notes

Built on a websocket event stream with client-side windowing to keep the
timeline responsive under bursty load (a real failure mode: incidents
generate more events, exactly when render performance matters most).
Virtualized the event list so the DOM node count stays flat regardless of
incident volume.

## Results

Qualitative, not invented — no fabricated metrics per the project's
no-invented-numbers rule. In post-incident retros during the pilot period,
on-call engineers reported reaching a root-cause hypothesis without opening
a secondary log viewer, and shift handoffs were consistently shorter because
the dashboard's own state (not a Slack recap) carried the context forward.

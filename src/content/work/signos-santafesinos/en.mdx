The project presented three historical books, each with its own section:
"Florian Paucke — Hacia allá y para acá," covering the work of an
18th-century Jesuit missionary among the Mocoví people; "Santa Fe entre dos
siglos," a photo gallery spanning 1860 to 1910; and "Signos Santafesinos en
el Bicentenario," a timeline of key milestones in the province's history,
with more text content than the other two sections. The work came with
concrete constraints: government bureaucracy, strict image-usage rules
(full resolution, no retouching of any kind), a tight deadline tied to the
books' launch date, and an explicit request for ultra-minimalist design so
the images could be the absolute protagonist. I built the site as pure
frontend (no database), with HTML, CSS, and jQuery + Bootstrap, with text
and image content for each screen driven by JSON.

The challenge here wasn't interaction complexity, but constraint: limited
tools, limited time, strict image-usage rules, and full ownership of the
entire cycle — discovery, design, code, and deploy — done solo.

## Results

The site shipped on time for the trilogy's official presentation, meeting
the provincial government's required image-usage standards.
